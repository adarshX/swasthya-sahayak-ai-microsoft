# Malaria and Fever — NVBDCP Guidelines
Source: National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP) 2023

## When to Suspect Malaria
Any person with FEVER (especially intermittent/cyclical) in a malaria-endemic area should be tested.

## Key Symptoms
- Fever (often cyclical — every 48-72 hours)
- Severe headache
- Chills and rigors (shaking)
- Body aches, muscle pain
- Nausea and vomiting
- Sweating after fever episode

## Severe Malaria Signs — URGENT REFERRAL
- High fever with altered consciousness / confusion
- Unable to eat or drink
- Repeated vomiting
- Seizures / convulsions
- Dark/cola-colored urine
- Severe anemia (very pale)
- Jaundice (yellow eyes)
- Heavy bleeding

## Triage Decision
- **URGENT REFERRAL (RED)**: Any severe malaria sign → Refer immediately. Give first dose of artemether-lumefantrine if available.
- **PHC VISIT (ORANGE)**: Fever with headache/vomiting → Needs blood test (RDT/microscopy) at PHC. Advise tepid sponging for fever.
- **HOME CARE (GREEN)**: Mild fever only, no danger signs → Give paracetamol. Monitor for 2 days. Return if fever persists or worsens.

## What ASHA Worker Should Do
1. If RDT kit available: Perform rapid test. Follow color code result.
2. If RDT not available: Refer to PHC for blood smear.
3. Give paracetamol for fever control (NOT aspirin in children).
4. Encourage fluids — ORS, water, buttermilk.
5. Advise to sleep under insecticide-treated bed net.

## RDT (Rapid Diagnostic Test) Instructions
1. Clean finger with alcohol swab
2. Prick with lancet, collect blood in pipette
3. Add blood to test card well
4. Add 2 drops of buffer solution
5. Wait 15-20 minutes, read result
6. Two lines = Positive (Pf or Pv), One line = Negative

## Malaria Prevention
- Sleep under long-lasting insecticidal net (LLIN)
- Use mosquito repellent
- Wear full-sleeved clothes in evening
- No stagnant water near house
- Indoor residual spraying (IRS) as per schedule
- Pregnant women: extra protection, chloroquine prophylaxis where recommended

## Dengue vs Malaria Differentiation
- Dengue: High fever, severe body pain, rash, bleeding gums/nose
- Malaria: Cyclical fever, chills, rigor
- Both need blood test for confirmation — refer to PHC
