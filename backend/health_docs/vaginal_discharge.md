# Vaginal Discharge — NHM Reproductive Health Guidelines
Source: National Health Mission (NHM) 2023

## Normal vs Abnormal Discharge
### Normal vaginal discharge:
- Clear or whitish
- No bad smell
- No itching or pain
- Amount may vary with menstrual cycle

### Abnormal — needs medical attention:
- Yellow, green, or grey color
- Foul smell or fishy odor
- Itching or irritation
- Pain during urination or intercourse
- Associated with fever or lower abdominal pain

## Common Causes
- **Bacterial Vaginosis**: Grey/white discharge with fishy smell
- **Candidiasis (yeast infection)**: White, thick (like curd), with intense itching
- **Trichomoniasis**: Yellow-green, frothy, foul-smelling
- **Sexually transmitted infections**: Gonorrhea, chlamydia — may have pus-like discharge
- **Cervical infection**: May have blood-streaked discharge

## Triage Decision
- **URGENT REFERRAL (RED)**: Discharge + high fever + severe abdominal pain → Possible pelvic inflammatory disease. Needs hospital care.
- **PHC VISIT (ORANGE)**: Abnormal discharge + fever OR pain → Needs examination and antibiotic treatment. Same-day referral.
- **HOME CARE (GREEN)**: Mild abnormal discharge, no fever/pain → Hygiene counseling + PHC visit within 3 days.

## Stigma-Safe Approach (for ASHA Worker)
This is a sensitive topic. Many women won't discuss it openly.

1. **Use private mode**: Have the patient answer questions directly on phone screen
2. **Normalize the conversation**: "Many women have this problem, it is very common and treatable"
3. **Don't be judgmental**: Do not ask about sexual partners or make assumptions
4. **Ensure privacy**: Conduct assessment in a private space, not in front of family
5. **Use respectful language**: Use medical terms the patient is comfortable with

## What ASHA Worker Should Advise
1. **Maintain genital hygiene**: Wash with plain water, no douching
2. **Wear cotton underwear**, change daily
3. **Do NOT self-medicate** with antibiotics
4. **Both partners** may need treatment for STIs
5. **Complete full antibiotic course** once prescribed
6. **Follow up** if symptoms don't improve in 7 days

## STI Screening
- All women with abnormal discharge should be offered STI screening
- Partner notification and treatment is important
- Condom use for prevention
- HIV testing should be offered (with counseling)

## In Pregnancy
- Abnormal discharge during pregnancy needs immediate PHC referral
- Can cause premature delivery, low birth weight, infection to baby
- Treat promptly — safe antibiotics available for pregnancy
