# General Danger Signs in Children Under 5 — WHO-IMNCI Protocol
Source: WHO Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMNCI) 2023

## What are General Danger Signs?
These are signs that indicate a child is critically ill regardless of the specific disease. ANY general danger sign means URGENT REFERRAL.

## The Four General Danger Signs
1. **Unable to drink or breastfeed**
   - Child cannot suck or swallow
   - Put water/milk to lips — if child cannot take in any fluid, this is a danger sign
   - Vomits everything immediately

2. **Vomits everything**
   - Cannot keep anything down
   - Offer fluid — if child vomits it all back, this is a danger sign
   - Different from occasional vomiting

3. **Convulsions (during this illness)**
   - Jerking movements of arms/legs, body becomes stiff
   - May lose consciousness
   - Eyes may roll up
   - Ask mother: "Has the child had fits/convulsions during this illness?"

4. **Lethargic or unconscious**
   - Child is abnormally sleepy, cannot be woken
   - Does not respond to sound or touch
   - Floppy, limp body
   - Does not look at mother's face or follow objects

## Triage Decision
- **URGENT REFERRAL (RED)**: ANY one general danger sign present → Refer immediately to hospital. Give pre-referral treatment.
- Do NOT wait to assess further if danger sign found — refer immediately.

## Pre-Referral Treatment (what ASHA can do before transport)
1. If convulsing: Place child on side, clear airway, protect from injury
2. If fever: Give paracetamol (NOT aspirin in children)
3. If dehydrated: Give ORS sips if child can drink
4. If unable to drink: Position child for NG tube at hospital (do not attempt in field)
5. Keep child warm — skin-to-skin if infant
6. Give first dose of oral antibiotic if available
7. NOTE THE TIME of onset and transport

## Common Causes
- Severe pneumonia
- Severe malaria (cerebral)
- Severe dehydration (from diarrhea)
- Meningitis
- Severe malnutrition with complications
- Sepsis (blood infection)

## Important for ASHA Worker
- Check for general danger signs FIRST in every sick child visit
- Ask mother specifically about: feeding, vomiting, convulsions
- Watch child's behavior: alertness, eye contact, response to voice
- If in doubt about any sign, assess as RED and refer
- Record time of assessment and symptoms observed
- Contact PHC/ambulance immediately (108/102)
