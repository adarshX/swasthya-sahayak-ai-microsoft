# Tuberculosis (TB) — NTEP Guidelines for ASHA Workers
Source: National TB Elimination Programme (NTEP) 2023

## When to Suspect TB
- Cough for more than 2 weeks
- Fever for more than 2 weeks (especially evening fever)
- Significant weight loss
- Night sweats
- Blood in sputum (hemoptysis)
- Loss of appetite
- Chest pain

## What ASHA Worker Should Do
1. If anyone has cough for 2+ weeks → Refer to nearest DMC (Designated Microscopy Centre) for sputum test
2. Collect 2 sputum samples if trained (morning sample is best)
3. Counsel patient about free treatment under NTEP
4. Emphasize: TB is CURABLE with proper treatment

## DOTS (Directly Observed Treatment)
- ASHA serves as DOT provider
- Ensure patient takes medicine regularly
- Treatment duration: 6 months minimum
- Do NOT stop treatment even if patient feels better
- Missing doses leads to drug resistance (MDR-TB)

## TB Treatment Phases
### Intensive Phase (2 months):
- 4 drugs: HRZE (Isoniazid, Rifampicin, Pyrazinamide, Ethambutol)
- Daily fixed-dose combination tablets

### Continuation Phase (4 months):
- 2 drugs: HR (Isoniazid, Rifampicin)
- Daily tablets

## Nikshay Poshan Yojana
- TB patients get Rs 500/month for nutrition support
- ASHA should help register patient on Nikshay portal
- Payment via DBT to patient's bank account

## Contact Tracing
- All household members of TB patient should be screened
- Especially children under 5 and immunocompromised
- Children under 5: May need IPT (Isoniazid Preventive Therapy2)

## TB + HIV
- All TB patients should be tested for HIV
- All HIV patients should be screened for TB
- Treatment for both can be given simultaneously

## Infection Control at Home
- Patient should cover mouth while coughing
- Good ventilation in house
- Separate sleeping area not mandatory if on treatment
- After 2 weeks of treatment, patient is much less infectious
