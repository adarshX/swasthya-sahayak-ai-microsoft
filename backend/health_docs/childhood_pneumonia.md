# Childhood Pneumonia — WHO-IMNCI Protocol
Source: WHO Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMNCI) 2023

## When to Suspect Pneumonia
A child under 5 years with COUGH or DIFFICULTY BREATHING should be assessed for pneumonia.

## Key Signs
- **Fast breathing**: The most important single sign of pneumonia in children.
  - Under 2 months: 60+ breaths per minute
  - 2-12 months: 50+ breaths per minute
  - 12 months to 5 years: 40+ breaths per minute
- **Chest indrawing**: The lower chest wall goes in when the child breathes in. Sign of severe pneumonia.
- **Stridor**: Harsh sound when child breathes in. Sign of severe obstruction.
- **Danger signs**: Unable to drink, persistent vomiting, convulsions, lethargic/unconscious.

## Triage Decision
- **URGENT REFERRAL (RED)**: Chest indrawing OR any general danger sign → Refer URGENTLY to hospital. Give first dose of antibiotic before referral.
- **PHC VISIT (ORANGE)**: Fast breathing without chest indrawing → Give oral amoxicillin for 5 days. Follow up in 2 days.
- **HOME CARE (GREEN)**: Cough without fast breathing → No antibiotic needed. Soothe throat with safe remedy. Follow up in 5 days if not improving.

## Home Care Advice for ASHA Worker
1. Continue breastfeeding/feeding. Small frequent meals.
2. Keep child warm. Increase fluids.
3. Clear nose if blocked — use saline drops.
4. Return IMMEDIATELY if: child gets breathing difficulty, fast breathing, unable to drink, becomes sicker.
5. Do NOT give cough suppressants to children under 5.

## Risk Factors That Increase Severity
- Severe malnutrition (increases risk by 1.5x)
- Previous pneumonia within 30 days (increases risk by 1.3x)
- HIV positive child
- Low birth weight
- Not breastfed
- Indoor air pollution (cooking with biomass fuel)

## Prevention
- Complete immunization (especially measles, pertussis, Hib vaccines)
- Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months
- Adequate nutrition (Vitamin A supplementation)
- Reduce indoor air pollution
- Handwashing with soap
