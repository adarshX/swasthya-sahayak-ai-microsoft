# Anemia Screening — NHM Anemia Mukt Bharat Guidelines
Source: National Health Mission (NHM) Anemia Mukt Bharat Programme 2023

## What is Anemia
Anemia is a condition where hemoglobin (Hb) in blood is lower than normal, reducing the blood's ability to carry oxygen.

## Who is at Risk
- Pregnant women (especially in rural India — prevalence >50%)
- Children 6-59 months
- Adolescent girls (10-19 years)
- Women of reproductive age
- Malnourished individuals

## Key Signs to Identify
- **Pallor**: Pale inner eyelids (pull down lower lid), pale nail beds, pale tongue
- **Weakness/fatigue**: Easily tired during normal activities
- **Breathlessness**: Getting out of breath during mild exertion
- **Dizziness/lightheadedness**
- **Fast heartbeat (palpitations)**
- **Swollen feet** (in severe cases)

## Hemoglobin Cutoffs (g/dL)
### Children 6 months - 5 years:
- Normal: ≥11
- Mild: 10-10.9
- Moderate: 7-9.9
- Severe: <7

### Pregnant women:
- Normal: ≥11
- Mild: 10-10.9
- Moderate: 7-9.9
- Severe: <7

### Non-pregnant women:
- Normal: ≥12
- Mild: 11-11.9
- Moderate: 8-10.9
- Severe: <8

## Triage Decision
- **URGENT REFERRAL (RED)**: Severe anemia (Hb <7), OR pallor + breathlessness + swollen feet → Refer for blood transfusion. Do not delay.
- **PHC VISIT (ORANGE)**: Moderate anemia (Hb 7-10), OR pallor + weakness → Refer to PHC for Hb test and iron treatment.
- **HOME CARE (GREEN)**: Mild pallor only → Start IFA tablets. Dietary counseling. Follow up monthly.

## IFA (Iron Folic Acid) Treatment
### Children 6-59 months:
- Prophylaxis: 20mg iron + 100mcg folic acid daily
- Treatment: 3mg/kg/day elemental iron for 3 months

### Pregnant women:
- One IFA tablet (60mg iron + 500mcg folic acid) daily for 180 days
- Start from 14 weeks of pregnancy
- Severe anemia: 2 tablets daily + refer to PHC

### Adolescent girls:
- Weekly IFA tablet (under WIFS programme)
- One tablet per week for 52 weeks

## Iron-Rich Foods to Advise
- Green leafy vegetables: spinach (palak), fenugreek (methi), amaranth (chaulai)
- Jaggery (gur/gud)
- Dates (khajoor)
- Pomegranate (anar)
- Ragi/finger millet
- Pulses and legumes (dal, chana, rajma)
- Eggs, fish, liver (if non-vegetarian)
- Iron-fortified foods (double-fortified salt, iron-fortified wheat flour)

## Important Tips for ASHA Workers
1. IFA tablets should be taken with food to reduce nausea
2. Take with citrus fruit/lemon water (Vitamin C increases iron absorption)
3. Do NOT take with tea or coffee (tannins reduce absorption)
4. Deworming tablet (Albendazole) every 6 months for children
5. Black/dark stool is normal side effect of iron tablets — counsel the patient
6. Monthly follow-up with Hb testing if available
