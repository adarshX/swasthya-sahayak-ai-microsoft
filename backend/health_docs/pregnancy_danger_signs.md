# Pregnancy Danger Signs — NHM High-Risk Pregnancy Guidelines
Source: National Health Mission (NHM) India, 2023

## Critical Danger Signs — Immediate Referral Required
Any of these signs in a pregnant woman requires IMMEDIATE hospital referral:

1. **Vaginal bleeding** (any amount during pregnancy)
   - First trimester: May indicate ectopic pregnancy or miscarriage
   - Third trimester: May indicate placenta previa or abruption
   - ACTION: Do NOT do vaginal examination. Refer immediately.

2. **Severe headache with blurred vision**
   - May indicate pre-eclampsia/eclampsia
   - Especially dangerous if with swollen face/hands
   - Check BP if available: ≥140/90 mmHg is high

3. **Convulsions/fits**
   - Eclampsia — life-threatening emergency
   - Place woman on left side, protect from injury
   - Refer URGENTLY — do not delay

4. **High fever** (>38°C / 100.4°F)
   - May indicate malaria, UTI, or sepsis
   - Give paracetamol and refer

5. **Severe abdominal pain**
   - May indicate ruptured ectopic, abruption, or obstructed labor
   - Do NOT give pain medication, refer immediately

6. **Water breaking without labor starting (premature rupture)**
   - Risk of infection if prolonged
   - Note time of rupture, refer to hospital

7. **Baby not moving** (reduced fetal movement after 28 weeks)
   - Ask: Has baby moved less than 10 times in 12 hours?
   - Refer for monitoring

## Antenatal Care Schedule
- First visit: Before 12 weeks (register pregnancy, blood tests, weight, BP)
- Second visit: 14-26 weeks
- Third visit: 28-34 weeks
- Fourth visit: 36 weeks onwards (weekly until delivery)

## What ASHA Worker Should Check at Every Visit
1. Blood pressure (if BP cuff available)
2. Weight gain (expected: 9-12 kg over pregnancy)
3. Fetal movement (after 28 weeks)
4. Signs of anemia: pallor, fatigue, breathlessness
5. Swelling of face, hands, or feet (pre-eclampsia)
6. Ask about vaginal bleeding, headache, fever

## High-Risk Pregnancy Markers
- Age under 18 or over 35
- Height under 145 cm
- Previous C-section or difficult delivery
- Previous stillbirth or neonatal death
- Rh-negative blood group
- Gestational diabetes
- Twin/multiple pregnancy
- Severe anemia (Hb < 7 g/dL)

## Iron-Folic Acid (IFA) Supplementation
- All pregnant women: 1 IFA tablet daily for 180 days
- Start at 14 weeks of pregnancy
- If severe anemia: 2 tablets daily + refer to PHC
- Take with food to reduce nausea
- Do NOT take with tea/coffee (reduces absorption)

## Delivery Planning
- Every pregnant woman should have an identified hospital for delivery
- Keep referral transport number ready
- Keep blood donors identified (especially for Rh-negative)
- JSY (Janani Suraksha Yojana) benefit: ensure registration
