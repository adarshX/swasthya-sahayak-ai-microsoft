# ASHA Worker General Knowledge — NHM Training Guidelines
Source: National Health Mission ASHA Training Modules 2023

## Who is an ASHA Worker?
ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist) is a trained community health volunteer selected from the village itself. She is the first point of contact for any health-related need in the community.

## ASHA Worker Responsibilities
1. **Maternal Health**: Identify pregnant women, ensure registration, accompany for ANC visits and delivery
2. **Child Health**: Growth monitoring, immunization tracking, identification of sick children
3. **Family Planning**: Counsel on contraception, distribute condoms/OCP, refer for sterilization
4. **Communicable Diseases**: Malaria RDT, TB case follow-up, refer suspected cases
5. **Health Education**: Nutrition counseling, hygiene promotion, sanitation
6. **Referral**: Identify danger signs and refer to PHC/hospital

## When to Refer vs Treat at Home

### ALWAYS REFER (RED) - Do not attempt home treatment:
- Unconscious or convulsing patient
- Severe bleeding (pregnancy or injury)
- Chest indrawing in child
- Large burn or wound
- Snake/scorpion bite
- Poisoning
- Severe dehydration (unable to drink)

### REFER SAME DAY (ORANGE):
- Fever for 3+ days
- Painful urination with fever
- Pregnant woman with headache/swelling
- Child not gaining weight for 2 months
- Abnormal vaginal discharge with pain

### HOME CARE (GREEN) - with follow-up:
- Mild fever (1-2 days)
- Mild cough without fast breathing
- Mild diarrhea without dehydration
- Minor wound (clean and bandage)

## Key Health Programmes ASHA Supports
- **JSY (Janani Suraksha Yojana)**: Cash incentive for institutional delivery
- **JSSK (Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram)**: Free delivery + treatment for mother and newborn
- **RBSK (Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram)**: Child health screening
- **NIS (National Immunization Schedule)**: Ensure complete vaccination
- **AMB (Anemia Mukt Bharat)**: IFA distribution, anemia screening
- **NTEP (National TB Elimination Programme)**: TB case finding, DOTS support
- **NVBDCP**: Malaria prevention, bed net distribution, IRS support

## Emergency Contact Numbers
- **108**: Emergency ambulance (most states)
- **102**: Referral transport for pregnant women
- **104**: Health helpline
- **1098**: Childline (for child emergencies)
- PHC number: Should be saved in ASHA's phone

## Monthly Reporting
ASHA must report to PHC monthly:
- Number of home visits
- Number of ANC women tracked
- Deliveries (institutional vs home)
- Immunization status of children
- Deaths (maternal, neonatal, under-5)
- Disease surveillance (fever, diarrhea, TB suspects)
